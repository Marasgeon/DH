The Denavit–Hartenberg Convention

Wherein a manipulator is reduced to four numbers per joint, those numbers to four elementary motions, and the motions to a single chain of matrix products — with a working model the reader may turn.

❦

The method set down by Denavit and Hartenberg in 1955 answers one stubbornly practical question: how should a coordinate frame be fastened to each link of a chain, so that the relation between neighbouring frames collapses into four numbers? Their answer is a table of four parameters per joint, one matrix per link, and a product that carries the base frame out to the tool. Change a number below and the whole apparatus — drawing, matrices, tool pose — follows at once.

§ IV

The Chain of Transformations

Multiply the link matrices in order and each product gives the pose of one more frame in the base. The last product is the tool pose — the whole forward kinematics of the arm in a single matrix.

§ V

A Check by Hand

A matrix product is easy to trust and hard to believe. When every twist αi vanishes, the chain lies in parallel planes, and the tool position may be written out by ordinary trigonometry. The two routes should agree.

§ VI

Notes on the Convention

What the convention fixes

This page uses the standard (or distal) convention: frame {i} is fastened to link i, its zi−1 axis lies along the axis of joint i, and its xi axis lies along the common normal from zi−1 to zi. The link transform is then

Ai = Rz(θi) · Tz(di) · Tx(ai) · Rx(αi)

so that T0n = A1A2⋯An. The modified (or proximal) convention reorders the same four motions and numbers the parameters differently; matrices from the two conventions are not interchangeable. State which one is in use before comparing results.

Origin of the methodJ. Denavit and R. S. Hartenberg, “A kinematic notation for lower-pair mechanisms based on matrices,” Journal of Applied Mechanics, 22(2):215–221, 1955.

How to read a homogeneous matrix

The upper-left 3×3 block is a rotation: its columns are the x, y, z axes of the new frame, written in the old one. The upper-right 3×1 column is the position of the new origin. The bottom row is always 0 0 0 1, which is what lets a chain of motions be carried by a single product.

T = [ R | p ; 0 0 0 | 1 ] — rotation, position, and the row that keeps the arithmetic homogeneous.

Exercises for the student

  1. Turn the first joint through a full revolution. Which entries of A1 move, and which refuse to? Explain both facts from the four elementary motions.
  2. Set a1 = 0 and α1 = 90°. Where do the axes z0 and z1 now meet, and what does d1 measure?
  3. Make the second joint prismatic. Which entry of A2 carries the variable, and why does the arm slide instead of swing?
  4. Load a planar preset and read § V. Do the closed form and the matrix product agree? Then set α1 = 90° and see why the check no longer applies.
